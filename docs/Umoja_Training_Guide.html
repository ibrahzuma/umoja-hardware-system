Umoja Hardware System — User Training Guide

For trainers and new users · Production: https://umoja.ehub.co.tz · Mobile: Umoja Pro (Android) · Edition of 13 September 2026 — covers the cash desk, Sales Accounting, vouchers & the General Ledger, the three statements, bulk import and the CRM register.

This guide is written to be delivered as a training session. Work through it in order:

  1. Part A — Getting started (everyone): sign in, find your way around, the golden rule of roles.
  2. Part B — Your role: each role has its own chapter — including the Cashier and a much longer Accountant chapter (the books). Find yours and read it end to end.
  3. Part C — The big flows: how a purchase, a sale, money going out, the till reaching the books, and back-dated work move through several roles. This is the part most people get wrong, so spend time here.
  4. Part D — Mobile app, troubleshooting, and a hands-on checklist you can run live with trainees.

How to use this in a classroom: project this file, sign in as each role using the test logins your administrator provides, and have each trainee do the "Try it now" exercise at the end of their chapter on the live system.

Part A — Getting started (everyone)

A1. Sign in

  1. Open https://umoja.ehub.co.tz in a web browser (Chrome or Edge recommended).
  2. Type your username and password.
  3. Click Sign in.

Your administrator creates your account and gives you the first password. Change it from Profile after your first login.

A2. The dashboard and the sidebar

After signing in you land on the Dashboard. The cards at the top tell you what needs attention right now (today's revenue, low-stock items, orders waiting, pending approvals). The left sidebar is your menu.

Your own work comes first. Under Overview the sidebar opens with My Workspace — the menu for your role — and only then the shared sections (Catalog, Commerce, CRM, Finance, HR, Administration). So an Afisa Ugavi finds Purchase Orders at the top of the menu, not buried below stock and sales. Opening one menu closes the others.

A3. The golden rule: you only see what your role allows

The menu changes depending on who you are. A Sales Rep does not see Finance. An Accountant does not see Dispatch. If a colleague has a button you don't, that's by design — your role doesn't include that permission.

If a menu item or button is missing, it is almost always a permissions matter, not a bug. Ask your System Administrator to check your role.

A4. Sessions time out

For security, you are signed out after 20 minutes of inactivity, and always when you close the browser. Just sign in again. Cookies are locked down (HttpOnly/Secure) — this is normal.

A5. My attendance (everyone with a linked staff record)

Every signed-in user has a My attendance link at the top of the sidebar to clock in/out for the day. This only works if your login has been linked to your employee record (your administrator does this). See the HR chapters for the full attendance flow.

A6. Every table can be exported, and long tables page themselves

Any list with an Export CSV / Print bar above it (the cash desk, the vouchers register, the General Ledger, supplier accounts…) exports exactly what the filters show — including rows the pager has scrolled past — as a CSV that opens cleanly in Excel. Long tables show 10 rows at a time with 10 / 25 / 50 / 100 controls underneath; a table with ten rows or fewer is left as it is.

A7. Notifications

The bell at the top right is your inbox. The system writes a notification when something needs you: a short delivery to explain, a supplier payment approved or rejected, a petty cash request decided, a posted sale queried by Accounts. Click one to jump to the screen it is about.

A8. My petty cash (everyone)

Anyone can ask for petty cash — for a bus fare, a spare part, a courier. My petty cash (top of the sidebar) shows your own requests and lets you raise a new one: the amount, what it is for, and the branch. It goes Pending → Approved (by an Admin) → Issued (by the Cashier, when the money is in your hand), or Rejected with a reason. You only ever see your own requests; the Admin and the Cashier see everyone's because they act on them. The float only moves when the Cashier presses Done — a promise is not cash.

Part B — Roles at a glance

Role Who they are Main job in the system
System Administrator Owner / IT lead Set up the business; manage users & roles; decide every approval queue (deliveries, supplier payments, petty cash); can do everything
Manager General manager Oversight of products, sales, and reports
Procurement Officer (Afisa Ugavi) Buyer Manage suppliers, raise Purchase Orders, inbound trucks
Stock Controller Receiving clerk Receive goods (GRN) against POs, keep stock balances accurate
Store Keeper Warehouse staff Pick goods for dispatch, load the truck
Sales Representative Cashier / salesperson Take orders at POS, manage customers & quotations
Store Manager Floor supervisor Verify deliveries item by item, run dispatch, move stock between branches
Sales & Credit Manager Sales supervisor Approve/decline orders, chase credit customers, record payments received
Cashier Holder of the cash tin Petty cash float, hand out approved petty cash, record supplier payments and other payouts (the Admin approves them)
Accountant Bookkeeper Take sales into the books, post vouchers, run the General Ledger and the three statements, keep the CRM register, expenses, income, taxes
HR Officer HR administrator Maintain employee records, leave, attendance, documents
HR Manager Head of HR Everything HR Officer does, plus payroll, departments, performance & discipline
Staff General employee Self-service only (clock in/out, profile, petty cash requests)

Two separate fleets — don't mix them up: Trucks/Drivers (Inventory) are for goods coming in from suppliers. Vehicles (Sales) are for goods going out to customers. They are intentionally different lists.

Three things wait for an Admin. Short deliveries, supplier payments and petty cash requests are all requests until an Administrator says yes. Nobody else can approve their own work, and the queues are on the Admin's sidebar under Administration.

Part B — Role chapters

Each chapter follows the same shape: Who you are → What you can do → Your daily flow → Tips → Try it now.

1. System Administrator

Who you are: the owner or IT lead who sets the system up and keeps it healthy.

What you can do: everything. You configure the business once (branches, categories, users, roles), then mostly watch the dashboard for problems.

Your daily flow

  1. Sign in and review the Admin dashboard — orders to dispatch, low stock, pending approvals, today's revenue.
  2. Create branches — Inventory → Branches → Add branch. Every stock level and every sale is tied to a branch, so set these up first.
  3. Create categories — Inventory → Categories. Each category carries a commission % applied to every sale of its products. (Old sales keep their old rate even if you change it later.)
  4. Manage users — Users. Each user gets a role that decides their menu and permissions.
  5. Manage roles — Roles. Review which permissions each role grants.
  6. Delivery Approvals — Administration → Delivery Approvals. Short deliveries the Store Manager flagged and Afisa Ugavi has explained wait here for you. Confirm takes the goods that did arrive into stock and leaves the balance owing; Reject sends it back for a better explanation and banks nothing. Until you decide, none of that delivery is stock. Clear this screen daily — goods sitting here are goods the shop cannot sell.
  7. Supplier Payment Approvals — Administration → Supplier Payment Approvals. Every payment the Cashier records is a request. Approve makes it paid — only then does the order's balance fall and the money show in the Cash Flow. Reject with a decision note sends it back to the Cashier, who amends it and resubmits; your note and their reply stay on the record.
  8. Petty Cash Approvals — Administration → Petty Cash Approvals. Requests anyone in the business has raised for petty cash. Approve or Reject (with a reason). Approving does not move cash — the Cashier hands it over and presses Done, and that is when the float falls.
  9. Bank accounts — Finance → Banks. The accounts payments are made from. Each gets a ledger in the books automatically.
  10. Company settings — Settings. Company name, logo, TIN/VRN, currency (TZS), default tax rate (the VAT box on Sales/Purchase vouchers uses it) — these appear on receipts and invoices.

Tips - After installing the system, the administrator runs python manage.py create_roles once to seed all role groups and permissions. Re-run it whenever roles or permissions change. - The Reports menu shows inventory aging, profitability by category, and commission per sales rep. - Secrets (keys, DB password) live only in the server's .env file — never share them.

Try it now: create a test branch, a test category with 5% commission, and a test Sales Rep user. Then open each of the three approval queues and read what is waiting.

2. Manager

Who you are: a general manager who needs oversight without doing the day-to-day data entry.

What you can do: view and edit products; view, change and cancel sales; view sales and inventory reports; view/add/edit users.

Your daily flow 1. Review the dashboard for the day's numbers and anything flagged. 2. Products — check the catalog, prices, and stock health (Catalog → Products, Inventory). 3. Sales — review the sales list, follow up on anything stuck (Commerce → Sales). 4. Reports — read profitability, aging, and commission reports (Reports).

Tips - Managers see the Dispatch Board and Vehicles too — handy for covering for a Store Manager. - Use reports for decisions; leave the data entry to the specialist roles.

3. Procurement Officer (Afisa Ugavi)

Who you are: the person who orders goods from suppliers.

What you can do: manage suppliers; raise and edit Purchase Orders (POs); manage inbound trucks and truck allocations.

Your daily flow

  1. Open your dashboard (scoped to procurement tasks).
  2. Manage suppliers — Inventory → Suppliers. Add suppliers with contact info; each PO is tied to one supplier.
  3. Purchase Orders — Inventory → Purchase orders. The list shows open, received, and closed POs.
  4. Raise a new PO — pick the supplier, add line items (product + quantity + agreed price). The PO is tracked until goods arrive.
  5. Confirm the goods arrived — when the supplier delivers, open the PO and click Confirm. The order goes to the Store Manager, who counts it. You are saying "it has turned up", not "it is all here".
  6. Explain a shortfall — if the Store Manager finds items missing, the order comes back to you marked Discrepancy with a Add Comment button. Write what happened (supplier short-shipped, damaged in transit, back-ordered). Your comment goes to the Admin, who confirms or rejects it. If the Admin rejects it, the order returns marked Re-comment with the Admin's reason — answer it properly.
  7. Confirm the balance — an order the Admin part-confirmed sits as Partially Received, listing exactly what is still owed. When the rest is delivered, click Confirm Balance and it goes round again — the Store Manager then checks only the outstanding quantity.
  8. Supplier credit — if the Cashier has ever over-paid a supplier, the order form shows the credit that supplier holds for us, and the Cashiers are told when you raise an order for them, so the credit gets used.
  9. Inbound fleet — Inventory → Trucks (deliveries from suppliers), Drivers, Maintenance, and Transport for the cost of each trip.

Tips - Trucks here = inbound from suppliers. Vehicles (Sales) = outbound to customers. Don't mix them. - Raise the PO before the supplier ships — the delivery check matches against it line by line. - Confirming is not receiving. Nothing becomes stock because you clicked Confirm; the store still has to count it. - Chase the Partially Received list — those are goods you have paid for or ordered and not yet got.

Try it now: add a supplier, raise a PO for two products, and confirm it through to the Store Manager.

4. Stock Controller

Who you are: the receiving clerk who books incoming goods into the system.

What you can do: create Goods Received Notes (GRNs); view and adjust stock; view products. Receiving a GRN is the only thing that increases stock.

Your daily flow

  1. Open your dashboard (receiving + stock health).
  2. GRN list — Inventory → GRNs. One GRN per delivery received.
  3. Receive goods (new GRN) — Inventory → GRNs → Create. Pick the matching PO, then enter the quantities actually received per product. Stock is added to your branch the moment you save.
  4. Live stock — Inventory → Inventory. Current quantity per product per branch. A red badge = at or below the low-stock threshold.

Tips - If you received less than the PO quantity, that's fine — the PO stays open for the remainder. - Set sensible low-stock thresholds on each stock row so dashboard alerts fire before you run out.

Try it now: receive a GRN against the PO the Procurement Officer raised, and confirm stock went up.

5. Store Keeper

Who you are: warehouse staff who pick goods for dispatch.

What you can do: view stock and the sales you've been assigned; help verify GRNs; load goods at dispatch.

Your daily flow

  1. Open your dashboard (minimal — just what you need to pick orders).
  2. See assignments — Sales → Dispatch. Filter to orders assigned to you; each row shows the customer, products to pick, and the delivery vehicle.
  3. Cross-check the sale — Sales → Sales. Confirm customer, products and quantities before picking.

Tips - If an item is short, tell the Store Manager before dispatch — once dispatched, stock is deducted. - You can print a delivery note from the sale's actions menu (it has the customer's address).

6. Sales Representative

Who you are: the cashier or salesperson at the counter or on the phone.

What you can do: take orders at the POS; record payment (cash, mobile money, bank transfer, or credit); manage customers; create quotations. You submit orders — your manager approves and dispatches them.

Your daily flow

  1. Open your dashboard (Commerce-focused).
  2. Open the POS — Sales → POS. Search products, add to cart, pick a customer, record payment, submit. The sale becomes Pending approval. - No back-orders. The POS refuses any sale the branch cannot fill. "Available" is the stock on the shelf minus what is already promised on other pending and approved sales — so three reps cannot each sell the same last five bags. Services are never stock-limited. - Credit sale with a deposit — choose Credit, type the deposit taken now; the rest stays as the balance owed and the customer appears in Debtors until it is paid.
  3. Sales list — Sales → Sales. Your past sales with status (Pending, Approved, Dispatched, Cancelled) and amount paid vs balance.
  4. Customers — add new customers; each customer's history is one click away.
  5. Quotations — written price estimates that do not touch stock. Convert to a sale in one click when the customer commits.

Tips - Commission is calculated automatically from the product's category and frozen on the sale — later rate changes don't affect it. - If a product won't appear in search, it may be out of stock at your branch. If the POS refuses the quantity, the stock is there but already promised to another sale — check Order management. - Use Quotations for big-ticket items the customer wants to think about.

Try it now: create a quotation, then convert it into a sale and submit it.

7. Store Manager

Who you are: the floor supervisor who approves orders and runs dispatch.

What you can do: approve/decline pending orders; dispatch approved orders (assign a picker + vehicle); manage drivers, truck maintenance, and stock transfers between branches.

Your daily flow

  1. Open your dashboard — ready-to-dispatch count, low-stock count, active fleet.
  2. Deliveries to Verify — My Workspace → Deliveries to Verify. Every order Afisa Ugavi has confirmed is listed here. Open one order at a time — you cannot check them all on one screen, by design. Inside, work down the items one by one: click All N arrived, or Short and type how many actually came plus a note saying what is missing. The counter shows "2 of 3 items checked" and the Submit button stays locked until every item has been counted. - Everything arrived → stock goes up immediately and the order closes. - Anything short → the order goes to Afisa Ugavi to explain, then to the Admin. Nothing enters stock in the meantime, so do not tell the shop the goods are available yet. A balance delivery comes back to this screen showing only the items still owed.
  3. Order Management — approve or decline — Sales → Order management. Approve only after checking the customer is real and the price is right.
  4. Dispatch Board — assign & ship — Sales → Dispatch board. Pick an approved order → choose a Store Keeper to pick → choose a Vehicle (or type hired-lorry info) → click Dispatch.
  5. Track vehicles — Sales → Vehicles. See Active vs In-transit; mark a returned vehicle Active so it can be reused.
  6. Move stock between branches — Inventory → Transfer. Stock leaves the source branch and enters the destination.
  7. Stock In vs Out — Inventory → Reports → Stock movements. For each item, what came in (deliveries, GRNs, transfers in, adjustments) against what went out (dispatches, transfers out) over a period. Export the whole report to Excel, or one item alone from its card — the file is named after the item.
  8. Stock adjustments — Inventory → Stock adjustment, for counts, damage and write-offs, each with a reason and recorded against the person who made it.

Tips - Stock only moves at Dispatch, not at Approve. Approve just clears the order for shipping. - Count the delivery honestly. Marking a short delivery as complete puts stock in the system that is not on the shelf, and every later count will disagree with it. - If a vehicle stays "busy" after the driver returns, click Return to free it. - Decline quickly — the customer is usually still on the line.

Try it now: approve the Sales Rep's order, then dispatch it and confirm stock dropped.

8. Accountant

Who you are: the bookkeeper. The till, the cash desk and the stores generate the numbers; you take them into the books, keep the ledger straight, and read the statements.

What you can do: post every sale into the Sales Accounting ledger; write vouchers (Sales, Purchase, Receipt, Payment, Contra, Journal) and read the General Ledger and trial balance; maintain the chart of accounts; run the Profit & Loss, Cash Flow and Balance Sheet; keep the CRM customer register; record expenses (with receipt photos), other income and tax payments; read the debtors list and the reports. Everything you post is yours: nobody on the shop floor can touch the ledger, and nothing you post can stop a sale being approved or dispatched — the books mirror the business, they do not drive it.

Your menu is Accountant in the sidebar:

8.1 Sales Accounting — taking the till into the books

Every sale made at the till — cash, credit, part-paid — lands here as Pending, however it was settled. Nothing is revenue until you post it.

  1. Filter to Pending (the default) and work down the list. The cards show how many are waiting and their value.
  2. Post & record payment — one act does two jobs: it takes the sale into the books (and so into the P&L) and records the money as received. The form asks how it was paid (cash / bank / mobile / cheque / other) and for the invoice to be attached; only the amount you confirm counts as cash. A posted row freezes — its cost of sales and commission are fixed, so a later price change cannot move a profit already reported.
  3. Query — if a sale does not look right (wrong customer, odd price, no invoice), press Query and type why. The seller is notified and the row shows Queried. It can still be posted once sorted out.
  4. A sale cancelled or deleted after posting does not vanish — it stays, flagged for reversal, so the books never lose a number they once reported.

The till's "paid" is a claim, not a receipt. Sales Accounting is where money is confirmed. The Cash Flow statement and the Balance Sheet read your confirmations, not the till.

8.2 Vouchers — the double-entry books

Everything that is not a till sale enters the books as a voucher: a document whose debits equal its credits. There are six kinds, and each kind only allows certain ledgers on each side — the dropdowns enforce it:

Voucher Debit side offers Credit side offers Use it for
Sales (SV-) the customer, or a bank account / cash book income ledgers and Output VAT an invoice we raised — credit sale, cash sale, or part-paid
Purchase (PU-) purchases / stock / expense ledgers and Input VAT the supplier, or a bank account / cash book an invoice a supplier gave us
Receipt (RV-) bank accounts and cash books every ledger except bank/cash money coming in
Payment (PV-) every ledger except bank/cash bank accounts and cash books money going out
Contra (CV-) bank accounts and cash books bank accounts and cash books moving our own money (bank ↔ cash, bank ↔ bank)
Journal (JV-) any ledger any ledger adjustments, accruals, corrections

Entering a voucher — Accountant → Vouchers → New Voucher → (type):

  1. Date of Transaction is required. Description is free text.
  2. Pick the debit ledger and type the amount. The moment the two sides differ, the difference is written into the next empty amount field on the short side — you only pick its account. Type over it if you want a different split; another line opens for what is left. Keep going until Difference = 0.
  3. Add as many debit and credit lines as you need (+ Line). Remove one with ✕.
  4. The bar at the bottom always shows Total Debit, Total Credit and the Difference. Post Voucher stays disabled until they balance — and the server checks again, so an unbalanced voucher can never be posted.
  5. On posting, the voucher gets its number (RV-000012 and so on, one sequence per type) and one General Ledger entry per line is written at once.

Allocating to invoices. When you credit a customer on a Receipt (they paid us) or debit a supplier on a Payment (we paid them), their outstanding invoices or bills appear under the line: number, date, original amount, what is already paid or allocated, and what is outstanding. Type the amount against each one — or press oldest first — and the invoice's balance falls the moment the voucher posts. You can never allocate more than is owed, and never more than the line carries.

Sales and Purchase vouchers carry more, because they are the invoice:

  • Customer / Supplier — pick the party. Their ledger lands on the party side for you (a credit sale). For a cash sale just pick the bank account or cash book on that line instead. A deposit is both: a bank line for what was paid, the customer line for the rest.
  • Sales Invoice No. (or the supplier's Purchase Invoice No.) is required and unique; the EFD RCT No. is a separate field, also unique (per supplier on purchases). Both are searchable in the register, and the invoice number rides on every General Ledger row of the voucher.
  • VAT box — type the VAT-exclusive or the VAT-inclusive figure (the rate comes from Settings), press Fill, and the goods line and the VAT line are written for you; the difference lands on the party side.
  • Payment status — Cash, Bank, Credit or Partly Paid — is read off your lines, never typed, and shown live in the totals bar.
  • What a Sales voucher leaves on the customer is an open invoice like any till sale: a later Receipt is allocated against it (and a Purchase voucher against a Payment).

Sales and Purchase vouchers do not create a till sale or a purchase order. They are the books' record of an invoice — for back-dated work, for months before the system, for anything the till never saw.

Wrong voucher? Vouchers are never edited. Open it and press Cancel (with a reason): its entries leave the General Ledger, the document stays on record marked Cancelled, and any invoice it had settled is owed again.

8.3 Bulk Import — a spreadsheet of vouchers

Back-dated work arrives as a spreadsheet, not one voucher at a time. Vouchers → Bulk Import:

  1. Download the template. It has four sheets: Vouchers (where you type), Ledgers (optional), Ledger List (your current chart of accounts — copy codes or exact names from it) and How to fill.
  2. On Vouchers, one row per voucher line. Rows sharing a Ref (any text — 1, 2, or the invoice number) are one voucher, so the debits and credits of a Ref must add up. Columns: Ref, Type, Date, Ledger, Side (Dr/Cr), Amount, Description, Party, Invoice No, EFD No, Against, Narration. - On a Sales/Purchase row, write Customer or Supplier in the Ledger column to mean the Party's own ledger. - On a Receipt row crediting a customer (or a Payment row debiting a supplier), Against names the invoice or bill it settles — a till invoice number, PO-12, or a Sales/Purchase voucher's invoice number.
  3. On Ledgers (optional), ledgers to create first, with opening balances and their side — a new bank account, Capital, Motor Vehicles. Customers and suppliers are not created here; name them on a voucher row and tick Create missing customers and suppliers if they are new.
  4. Check file. Every voucher is run through the same checks as the form; the report lists each one — rows, type, party, invoice, totals — with ready or the exact error (row 8: no ledger called "Nowhere Bank"). Nothing is kept.
  5. When every row reads ready, Post N vouchers. The file lands whole or not at all — one bad row and nothing posts, so a half-imported month cannot happen. Voucher numbers run in date order.

8.4 General Ledger and Ledgers

  • General Ledger — pick a ledger for its statement with opening balance, every entry (date, voucher, type, invoice, description, debit, credit) and a running balance; or leave it on Trial balance for every ledger at once, with opening, movements and closing Dr/Cr. The two closing columns always agree — that is what double entry buys. Date range and This month / Last month / This year / All time are at the top.
  • Ledgers (the chart of accounts) — every ledger has a kind (bank, cash book, customer, supplier, income, expense, asset, liability, tax, equity) and that kind is what puts it in the right dropdowns. Bank accounts, customers and suppliers get their ledger automatically the moment they exist elsewhere in the system — never create those by hand. Add your own income/expense/asset ledgers here, set opening balances (with the side), and close a ledger you no longer use rather than deleting it (a ledger with entries cannot be deleted).

8.5 The three statements

Statement What it reads Notes
Profit & Loss Revenue from posted sales only, less cost of sales, commission, expenses, petty cash payouts and other payments; plus other income; less tax payments Cash confirmed is shown beside revenue, never instead of it
Cash Flow Receipts dated by when you confirmed them, supplier payments, petty cash payouts, other payments, taxes Supplier payments made from credit are excluded — no money moved. Petty cash top-ups are an internal transfer, shown as a note
Balance Sheet Today only. Stock, debtors (invoices Accounts has not confirmed), cash (derived from the cumulative cash flow), creditors (every live purchase order) The system holds no capital, drawings or fixed-asset records, so the gap between net assets and retained earnings is shown as unrecorded — that is honest, not a bug

8.6 CRM — the customer register

CRM → Customer Records is a standalone register of who bought what and what they still owe. Every till sale syncs into it automatically with its paid / part-paid / credit state, and a payment recorded on the sales side updates the same row. You can also bulk-import rows from Excel/CSV (download the template on the page), export the register, and print a PDF statement for one customer or for the whole list. Fields people maintain — TIN, EFD receipt number, receipt number — are yours to fill and are never overwritten by the sync. Editing a CRM row never changes a sale.

8.7 Expenses, income, taxes, debtors

  1. Expenses — Finance → Expenses → Add expense. Pick a category, the amount, which bank account it was paid from, and attach the receipt photo.
  2. Other income — Finance → Income, for income that is not a product sale (rent, services).
  3. Tax payments — Finance → Taxes: type (VAT, PAYE, SDL, …), period, amount, and the TRA receipt number.
  4. Debtors — Debtors lists only sales that are still short, showing the outstanding amount. View opens the invoice; Record Payment enters money as it arrives.
  5. Bank accounts — Finance → Banks: the accounts expenses and payments are paid from. Each one also has a ledger in the books, kept in step automatically.
  6. Reports — aging, profitability by category, commission per rep.

Tips - Post the till daily. Until a sale is posted it is not revenue, its money is not cash, and the statements do not know it exists. - The difference bar is your friend: when the credit side is short, the next credit line opens with the gap already typed in. Pick the account and you are balanced. - Never total supplier payments without filtering to approved — a recorded payment is a request until the Admin approves it. The screens already do this for you. - One route per fact. A supplier's bill is either a purchase order (raised by Afisa Ugavi, paid by the Cashier) or a Purchase voucher (typed by you for back-dated work) — not both. - The two documents that define this module — Voucher Data Entry Requirements and Application Requirements for Back-dated Work — are implemented as written, with one correction: their example puts a 200,000 difference on a new Debit line; the rule (and the system) put it on the short side.

Try it now: post a Receipt of 1,000,000 into the bank, crediting a customer 800,000 (allocate it to one of their invoices) and Sales Revenue for the 200,000 the system carries to the second line. Then open the General Ledger, pick the bank, and find the entry.

9. Cashier

Who you are: the person who holds the cash tin. Money leaves the business through your hands — but only after somebody else has said yes.

What you can do: keep the petty cash float (top it up, record what goes out); hand over petty cash the Admin has approved; record supplier payments against purchase orders and resubmit ones the Admin sent back; record other payments that are neither a supplier invoice nor a tax; read the By Supplier account view. You cannot approve anything — every payment you record is a request until an Administrator approves it.

Your menu is Cashier in the sidebar.

Your daily flow

  1. Petty Cash — Cashier → Petty Cash. The cards show the float balance, cash in and cash out. Top Up Float records money put into the tin; Record Payment records a voucher going out (payee, category, branch, what for). Rows are grouped by month; the whole register exports to CSV.
  2. Hand over approved requests — the To Issue section on the same page lists petty cash requests an Admin has approved. Press Done only when the money is in the person's hand. That is the moment the float moves; a request that is approved but not issued has not touched the tin. If issuing would take the float negative the system refuses — top the float up first.
  3. Supplier Payments — Cashier → Supplier Payment. Pick the order being paid (the list is every order with a supplier that still has a balance, drafts included); the supplier comes from the order, never typed. Enter amount, method, reference, date, a note. It is saved Pending and goes to the Admin. - Approved → the balance on the order falls and the money shows in the Cash Flow. - Rejected → it comes back to you with the Admin's decision note. Open it, amend the entry (only pending and rejected rows can be edited), add your reply and Resubmit — it goes back as Pending. Both notes stay on the record for the whole loop. - Supplier credit — if you ever over-pay an order (10m paid on a 10m order plus another 10m), the supplier holds that money for us. The figure shows on the order form; spend it by recording a payment from credit against another of their orders. It draws the credit down and moves no cash.
  4. By Supplier — Cashier → By Supplier: for each supplier, what was ordered, what is paid (approved only), what is pending approval and what is still owed. Narrow the paid side to a month.
  5. Other Payments — Cashier → Other Payments: payouts that are neither a supplier invoice nor a tax (a refund, a casual labourer, a licence). Payee, category, method, amount, receipt reference.

Tips - Pending money is not paid money. Every total on your screens counts approved payments only; the pending figure is shown separately so you can chase it. - Petty cash requests are raised by the people who need the money (My petty cash), approved by the Admin, and issued by you. Three people, three moments — never short-cut it by recording a payment straight from the tin for something that was meant to be requested. - Export a month's petty cash to CSV before the count; it is what the filters show, nothing more.

Try it now: top the float up by 100,000, raise a petty cash request as another user, have the Admin approve it, press Done on it, and watch the float fall.

10. HR Officer

Who you are: the HR administrator who keeps employee records up to date.

What you can do: create, view, edit and delete employee records; view departments and job positions; manage leave requests; record attendance; upload employee documents; view performance reviews.

Payroll, departments setup, performance authoring and disciplinary actions are the HR Manager's job (next chapter).

Your daily flow 1. Open HR → HR Dashboard for an overview of headcount and pending items. 2. Employees — HR → Employees. Search and filter by status, department, or branch. 3. Add an employee — HR → Add Employee. Fill in identification (Employee #, names, NIDA/TIN/NSSF/NHIF), contact, employment (department, position, branch, hire date), compensation, and bank details. Save. 4. Delete an employee — on the employee list, use the trash button. - The system refuses to delete an employee who already has payslips and shows a clear message. In that case set their status to Terminated instead of deleting, so payroll history is preserved. 5. Leave requests — HR → Leave Requests. Record requests; an approver (HR Manager/Admin) approves or rejects. 6. Attendance — HR → Attendance. Record or correct daily attendance for staff. 7. Documents — upload contracts, IDs, certificates against each employee.

Tips - Prefer Terminated over delete for anyone who has ever been paid — it keeps a clean audit trail. - Fill in NIDA / TIN / NSSF / NHIF at onboarding; payroll relies on them later. - An employee can only clock themselves in/out (My attendance) if their login is linked to their employee record — your administrator sets that link.

Try it now: create a test employee, then try to delete one who has a payslip and read the message.

11. HR Manager

Who you are: the head of HR — everything the HR Officer does, plus the org chart, payroll, performance and discipline.

What you can do (in addition to HR Officer): create/edit departments, job positions and leave types; run payroll; manage payslips; author performance reviews; record disciplinary actions; approve/reject leave.

Your daily flow 1. Set up the org chart (once, then maintain) — HR → Departments and job positions. Assign a department head. 2. Leave types & approvals — define leave types (annual, sick, etc.); approve or reject leave requests. Approving a leave that starts today flips the employee to On leave. 3. Run payroll (monthly): 1. HR → Payroll → create a Payroll Period for the month/year (it starts as Draft). 2. Open the period → Generate payslips. The system creates one payslip per active employee and auto-computes Tanzanian statutory amounts: PAYE (TRA brackets), NSSF 10% (employee + matching employer), WCF 0.5%, SDL 4%, plus NHIF and other deductions you enter. The period becomes Processed. 3. Review/adjust individual payslips if needed (e.g. overtime, bonus, advances) — totals recalculate automatically. 4. Mark paid once salaries are disbursed → the period becomes Paid. 4. Performance reviews — HR → Performance. Record period, rating (1–5), strengths, areas to improve, goals. 5. Discipline — HR → Discipline. Record warnings, suspensions, terminations with reason and date.

Tips - Generate payslips is idempotent — re-running it on a period recomputes existing payslips rather than duplicating them. Safe to re-run after fixing salaries. - Deleting a payroll period removes all its payslips (admin-restricted, with a warning) — only do this on a draft you created in error. - PAYE is computed on gross minus NSSF, matching TRA's taxable-income definition. Brackets follow FY 2024/2025; update them if the government changes them.

Try it now: create a payroll period for the current month, generate payslips, open one and read the PAYE/NSSF breakdown, then mark the period paid.

12. Staff (general employee)

Who you are: an employee with a login but no specialist module access.

What you can do: self-service only — My attendance (clock in/out), My petty cash (raise a request and follow it), and Profile (update your name/email, change password). Everything else is hidden by design.

C2. Sale → Dispatch → Delivery (stock goes OUT)

This is the most important flow. Here is one real walk-in sale moving through every station:

# Role Action Result
1 Sales Rep Open POS, add products, pick customer, take payment, Submit Sale = Pending. Stock NOT moved.
2 Sales Rep Sale appears in Sales list as Pending Rep is free to serve the next customer
3 Store Manager Order management shows the order with Approve/Decline Manager verifies customer & price
4 Store Manager Approve Order cleared for dispatch. Stock STILL not moved.
5 Store Manager Dispatch board → assign Store Keeper + Vehicle Order ready to ship
6 Store Manager Dispatch Stock IS deducted now. Status = Dispatched, vehicle = In transit, invoice + delivery note generated
7 Store Keeper Pull goods, load the assigned truck Goods leave the warehouse
8 Accountant Record the fuel/freight expense behind the trip (receipt attached) Cost captured against the month
9 Accountant Sales Accounting → Post & record payment (method + invoice) The sale is now revenue in the P&L and cash in the Cash Flow — see C5

The two facts everyone must remember: - Stock moves at Dispatch (step 6), not at Approve (step 4). - Commission is frozen at sale creation (step 1) based on the product's category. Changing the category rate later does not change historical sales. - If a sale is later deleted, its stock is restored automatically.

Part D — Mobile app, troubleshooting, and exercises

D1. Mobile companion app — Umoja Pro (Android)

Umoja Pro signs in with the same username/password as the website and shares the same data.

  1. Sign in — open Umoja Pro, enter your credentials, tap Sign in. The app remembers your token so you stay signed in across launches. Sign out from the profile menu in the app bar.
  2. Four tabs at the bottom: - POS — open a new sale, search products, take payment. - Stock — current stock per branch with low-stock alerts. - Quotes — list of quotations; tap to convert to a sale. - Customers — directory; add customers; view a customer's sales history.
  3. Same backend, live updates — sales created on the phone appear in the Manager's Order Management on the website immediately; stock changes broadcast in real time so other devices update without refreshing.

Mobile tips - If Sign in seems to do nothing, the build is probably pointing at the wrong API. Reinstall a build configured for https://umoja.ehub.co.tz. - Pull down to refresh any tab. - Sign out from the profile menu to clear the saved token.

Quick reference

  • Web: https://umoja.ehub.co.tz · Mobile: Umoja Pro (Android)
  • Session timeout: 20 minutes of inactivity
  • Stock rises: at GRN / delivery check. Stock falls: at Dispatch. No back-orders: a sale the branch cannot fill is refused at the POS.
  • Commission: auto, frozen at sale creation, by product category.
  • Three approval queues (Admin): Delivery Approvals · Supplier Payment Approvals · Petty Cash Approvals.
  • Money counts when: a supplier payment is approved; petty cash is issued (Done); a sale is posted in Sales Accounting.
  • Vouchers: SV Sales · PU Purchase · RV Receipt · PV Payment · CV Contra · JV Journal — post only when Total Debit = Total Credit; the difference fills the next line on the short side.
  • Bulk import: one row per line, grouped by Ref; whole file or nothing.
  • Payroll path: Draft → Processed (Generate) → Paid (Mark paid).
  • Missing a button? It's your role's permissions — ask the Administrator.
